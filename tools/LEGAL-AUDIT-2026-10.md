# Legal and factual audit, October 2026

Audit of every claim in the workbench and the handbook that names a law, article, section, date, threshold, penalty, regulator action or court outcome. Checked on 13 October 2026 against primary sources (statute text, regulators' own pages, court orders) where they could be reached. Read-and-report only; no repo file was changed.

**How it was checked, and the limits.** EUR-Lex returned empty pages or a bot challenge to every fetch, so EU article text was read on two independent mirrors (lawplayer.com, lexaris.io, eu-digital-services-act.com) and on Commission pages; anything marked "mirror" deserves a one-minute spot check on EUR-Lex before you rely on it. leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, congress.gov, nmdoj.gov, ag.ks.gov and Justia returned 403, so California statutes were read from the chaptered PDFs, and the New Mexico and Kansas items from the court's own order and from news that quotes the regulator. Ofcom and esafety pages are covered in the UK/AU section with their own notes. Statuses: **Correct** (confirmed against a source), **Outdated** (true when written, the law or the facts have moved), **Wrong** (not what the source says), **Unverifiable** (a real attempt did not settle it; what was tried is stated).

Where the suggested replacement is "—", the text can stay as it is.

### The ten corrections that matter most for someone relying on the tool

1. **Transparency report builder: 17 template categories, not 15** (`partTR.js:60-61, 228, 231`). The notices column is missing "Type of alleged illegal content not specified by the notifier", so a report built from the tool can't file a row the Commission's template requires.
2. **DSA Article 32 runs the other way** (`partDSA.js:180-181`). You must tell consumers who bought the illegal product in the six months *before* you learned of it; there is no six-month deadline to tell them.
3. **AI Act Article 50 in the world guide merges two duties and narrows one** (`partLearn.js:613`). Providers must mark all generative output, text included, not only realistic media; deployers disclose deepfakes at first exposure; "in force since" should be "applies from"; and pre-existing systems have until 2 December 2026 for the marking (Digital Omnibus, Reg (EU) 2026/1744).
4. **India's IT Rules changed on 20 February 2026** (`partE2.js:399-400`). Court or government takedown orders now carry a 3-hour clock (from 36), AI-generated content must be labelled with provenance markers, and law-firm summaries report 2-hour intimate-image and 7-day grievance deadlines. The tool still shows the 2021 timings.
5. **Brazil's ECA Digital is in force (17 March 2026) and its age-verification rule is narrower than stated** (`partE2.js:404-405`). Self-declaration is banned for content unsuitable for minors (Art 9), not for every service minors might use; the loot-box ban and parental tools are right.
6. **US state children's laws have moved** (`partE2.js:354-355`). California's design code is now only partly enjoined, SB 976's feed rules are live, Texas's App Store Accountability Act is enforceable, New York's SAFE for Kids rules apply from 25 January 2027, and Texas's SCOPE filtering duty stays blocked.
7. **DSA enforcement moved on** (`partDSA.js:48-52`; `ch16 l.18`). Temu was fined €200 million (28 May 2026) and AliExpress €550 million (20 July 2026); the four adult platforms received preliminary findings on 26 March 2026. The X fine is no longer the only decision.
8. **AB 2655 is on appeal, not dead** (`partLearn.js:616`). AB 2839 was struck on First Amendment grounds (29 Aug 2025, no appeal); AB 2655 was held preempted by Section 230 and California's Ninth Circuit appeal (No. 25-6138) is pending under a stipulated non-enforcement.
9. **Texas's ride-hail law requires an annual criminal check, not an annual driving-record check** (`partS13.js:129`).
10. **Ofcom's revised illegal content codes apply from 30 September 2026** (handbook ch05, ch07, ch15 link the Feb 2025 edition). The hash-matching measures changed; re-check the thresholds the handbook quotes and repoint the PDF link. Related: for 2025 the DSA transparency templates applied only from 1 July, so the tool's "calendar year 2025, due end of February 2026" is right only for VLOPs (`partTR.js:85-88`; `ch17 l.102`).

Smaller fixes worth making in the same pass: Canada's reporting Act was retitled in 2024 and preservation is 21 days (`partE2.js:394`); the glossary's VLOP threshold is "45 million or more", not "more than" (`partTSG.js:88`); PolicyLM-1.7B was listed in ROOST's Model Community, not "released with ROOST" (`partAbout.js:123`, `partLearn.js:123`); the Sora 2 product was discontinued on 26 April 2026 (`partLearn.js:208`); the COPPA "one year to comply" line is past tense now (`partS12.js:113`); the TAKE IT DOWN trigger is a "valid" request, not a "verified" one (`partLearn.js:614`); Epic's $275 million was the COPPA count, with default-on chat a separate unfairness finding (`partCP.js:156`, `partS10.js:19`).
